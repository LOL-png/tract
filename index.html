A track-conditioned world model (TWM, middle) rolls out the visual outcome for each candidate conditioned on its track, and a vision-language critic (VLAC, middle) selects the highest-reward rollout; the robot executes the paired action.

Abstract

Robot actions are embodiment-specific and only weakly aligned with image-space visual change, which limits their effectiveness as a conditioning signal for video world models. Visual tracks — the motion of task-relevant points through the scene — are largely embodiment-agnostic and specify motion directly in image space.

TrAct uses visual tracks as an intermediate interface between control and prediction. A vision-language-action-and-track policy proposes candidate actions paired with their corresponding tracks, a track-conditioned world model predicts the visual outcome induced by each track, and a vision-language critic scores the predicted outcomes against the language instruction. The action paired with the highest-scoring track is then executed by the robot.

On the LIBERO-INTEGRAL benchmark and real-world Franka manipulation, TrAct improves task success from 27% to 55% in simulation and from 49% to 76% on real-world tasks over the π0.5 baseline. Furthermore, the track-conditioned world model consistently improves video prediction quality over the action-conditioned world model.

Method

Overview of the TrAct framework. Given an observation and a language instruction, the vision-language-action-and-track model jointly predicts K candidate actions and their corresponding visual tracks. Each set of predicted tracks conditions a world model to generate future video frames. A vision-language-action critic scores the predicted outcomes and selects the action whose rollout best fits the instruction.

Track vs. Action-Conditioned World Model

We compare two world models that differ only in what they condition on: the track-conditioned world model (TWM), which is guided by the predicted point tracks, and the action-conditioned world model (AWM), which is guided by the raw robot action chunk.

The track-conditioned model produces more spatially accurate and motion-coherent frames than the action-conditioned one across both agent-view and wrist-view cameras. The action-conditioned model often exhibits incorrect gripper poses, disappearing objects, spatial drift, and cross-view object position inconsistencies, especially in wrist view.

Results

All variants share the same candidate generator and the same critic. They differ in the policy that proposes candidates, and in what the world model conditions on.

  • π0.5 — the baseline vision-language-action policy.
  • VLAT — a vision-language-action-and-track policy that jointly predicts actions and their tracks.
  • VLAT + AWM — VLAT candidates rolled out by the action-conditioned world model and scored by the critic.
  • TrAct — VLAT candidates rolled out by the track-conditioned world model and scored by the critic.

Each adjacent pair isolates one factor:

  • π0.5 → VLAT — jointly predicting tracks alongside actions.
  • VLAT → VLAT + AWM — adding world-model-based action selection.
  • VLAT + AWM → TrAct — the action-conditioned versus the track-conditioned world model.
